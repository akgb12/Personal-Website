# Information Architecture

## Navigation

Recommended nav:

`AK` | About | Experience | Projects | Research | Publication | Coursework | Contact

Behavior:
- fixed or floating
- transparent/minimal over hero
- gains a subtle backdrop or tonal shift over content
- smooth same-page anchor navigation
- active section indicator based on viewport
- keyboard accessible
- mobile collapses cleanly without becoming a giant menu overlay unless the design truly needs it

The nav should feel like part of the composition, not a conventional corporate navbar.

---

# Section 1 — About / Education

Purpose:
Establish identity quickly without a slogan.

Must include:
- Aney Kanji
- natural short About text
- Texas A&M University
- B.S. Computer Science dates
- B.S. Statistics dates
- Engineering Honors
- no GPA

Suggested composition:
- Hero consumes the first viewport.
- About/Education emerges from the hero rather than beginning as a separate card.
- Degrees can be split into two typographic blocks or two lines that enter independently.
- A large background word such as `TEXAS A&M` or `EDUCATION` may be used carefully.

---

# Section 2 — Experience

All nine experiences must be represented.

Preferred desktop pattern:
- pinned/sticky viewport
- organization image/logo anchors the scene
- role/company/date/location transform as scroll advances
- each role feels like a beat in one continuous timeline
- no bullet-list résumé layout
- short contextual sentence is optional and should never dominate

Possible visual structure:
- left: giant `EXPERIENCE` or timeline index
- center/right: active company mark
- metadata enters/exits independently
- subtle progress rail or year markers

Mobile:
- do not force a long pinned sequence
- use an elegant vertical timeline/list with generous spacing and motion-light reveals

---

# Section 3 — Projects

Five flagship projects:
1. Google Sapphire
2. Paladin
3. FrostSight
4. CloudGuard Audit Agent
5. Kung Fu Express

Preferred treatment:
- one project per scene
- Apple-product-launch feeling
- project title first
- concise project sentence
- visual/architecture layer
- technology icons enter with the scene
- repo/live links only where valid
- no identical card grid

Technology icons:
- no permanent visible text labels
- accessible tooltip/title/aria label is okay
- icons can form a rail, constellation, staggered cluster, or subtle orbit depending on project
- keep composition intentional

---

# Section 4 — Research

Three items, newest to oldest:
1. AI Systems Researcher
2. Flow Matching / Kestrel
3. Mathematics Research Assistant

This section should visually communicate a progression:
AI systems → ML research tooling → mathematical research.

Potential treatment:
- three-stage sticky research narrative
- diagrams or abstract architecture rather than company-style logo cards
- current AI-systems work can use an animated pipeline:
  `SOURCE → INGEST → CANONICAL REPRESENTATION → AGENT/ORCHESTRATION → VALIDATION → REMEDIATION`
- Kestrel can use velocity-field/trajectory visual language
- math can transition into Fibonacci sequence / decomposition typography

Do not use names of advisors unless necessary.

---

# Section 5 — Publication

Dedicated section for the single paper.

The design must make one publication feel intentional, not sparse.

Suggested hierarchy:
- small `PUBLICATION · 2025`
- oversized paper title
- formal citation details
- Journal / arXiv / DOI actions
- subtle mathematical/Fibonacci visual system

Build it as a reusable publication component so future papers can be appended.

---

# Section 6 — Coursework

Use a kinetic editorial treatment rather than a chip wall.

Preferred:
- ObsidianUI Text Stream or a heavily customized equivalent
- separate Graduate and Undergraduate streams/lanes if it improves clarity
- real semantic course list remains available to assistive technology and search indexing
- course codes can be secondary typography

Do not let the section feel like a transcript.

---

# Section 7 — Contact / Footer

Dramatic but clean ending.

Possible large text:
`Aney Kanji`
or
`Let's connect.`

Avoid “LET'S BUILD SOMETHING” if it feels like generic portfolio copy.

Include:
- Email
- LinkedIn
- GitHub
- Résumé

Footer can use small metadata and a subtle name interaction.
